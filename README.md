# Project Overview
The project analyzes the growth and distribution of electric vehicles across the United States while uncovering where EV adoption is happening fastest, how EVs compare to traditional fuels, and what insights can be drawn for policymakers and automakers.

# Objective
The goal is to understand
- What percentage of vehicles in each state are EVs, PHEVs, HEVs, and gasoline?
- Which states have the highest EV adoption rates, and which states lag behind?
- Which alternative fuels (biodiesel, ethanol, hydrogen) are significant vs. niche?
- Where should policymakers prioritize EV infrastructure investment based on adoption trends and gaps in support?

# Workflow
Data Cleaning
- Check for missing values, zeroes, and any potential data quality issues.
- Standardize state names if necessary.

Market Share Analysis
- Calculate the percentage of EVs, PHEVs, HEVs, and Gasoline vehicles for each state.
- Identify the top 5 states with the highest EV adoption rate (EVs as a % of all registered vehicles).
- Compare EV adoption in California vs. other large states (e.g., Texas, Florida, New York).

Trend & Insights
- Highlight which alternative fuels (biodiesel, ethanol, hydrogen) have meaningful presence vs. niche usage.
- Create visualizations (bar charts, pie charts, or maps) showing EV distribution by state.
- Summarize key insights: Which states are leading? Which lag behind? What does this imply for infrastructure planning?
  
# Key Insight
- Gasoline usage remains high across all 50 U.S. states. States such as California and Hawaii continue to show high gasoline-vehicle usage despite having some of the highest EV adoption rates in the country.
- California (3.41%), Hawaii (2.37%), Washington (2.25%), Nevada (1.85%), and New Jersey (1.84%) rank among the top five states for EV adoption in the U.S. 
- Among alternative fuels, ethanol has the most significant presence, particularly when compared with biodiesel. Hydrogen remains a niche category.

# Recommendation
While the top five states with the highest EV adoption rates present attractive opportunities for EV businesses, market entry decisions should also consider factors such as competition, operating costs, regulations, infrastructure, and overall market potential. From this perspective, states such as Texas and Florida could also be promising markets, given their growing EV adoption, large consumer markets, and relatively limited public transportation options. In addition, businesses should consider the meaningful presence and growing adoption of alternative fuels, particularly ethanol. States with strong ethanol usage may present opportunities for businesses exploring diversified and complementary clean-fuel solutions alongside EVs.
